## 💡 Pharmacology Tip 1: The A–C Gout Reversal

When answering a basic gout question, remember this simple **A ↔ C reversal**:

### **A = C**

**A**llopurinol → **C**hronic gout control

Allopurinol lowers uric acid and is used as long-term urate-lowering treatment to prevent future gout attacks and joint damage.

### **C = A**

**C**olchicine → **A**cute gout attack

Colchicine reduces the inflammation caused by urate crystals and may be used to treat an acute gout flare.

### 🧠 Memory Formula

> **Allopurinol begins with A but points to Chronic.**
> **Colchicine begins with C but points to Acute.**

```text
A → C
Allopurinol → Chronic control

C → A
Colchicine → Acute attack
```

### 🎯 Examination Tip

If a basic examination question mentions:

* **Long-term uric-acid reduction or prevention of recurrent attacks** → think **allopurinol**.
* **Painful acute gout inflammation** → consider **colchicine**, alongside other guideline-recommended options.

### ⚠️ Important Exception

This mnemonic is a memory aid—not an absolute prescribing rule.

* Colchicine may also be used temporarily to prevent flares when allopurinol or another urate-lowering treatment is started or increased.
* If a patient is already established on allopurinol, it should not normally be stopped solely because an acute gout flare develops.
* Acute gout may also be treated with an NSAID or a short course of an oral corticosteroid, depending on the individual patient.

> **Final memory line: A crosses to C, and C crosses to A—but always check the clinical context.**

## 💡 Pharmacology Tip: Earlier to Shorter, Later to Longer

For these **selected medicine pairs**, the drug or number appearing earlier may help you remember the shorter or faster option, while the later one represents the longer or slower option.

> **Earlier letter → shorter or faster**
> **Later letter → longer or slower**
> **Earlier number → shorter**
> **Later number → longer**

---

### 1. Ipratropium and Tiotropium

```text
I comes before T

Ipratropium → Short-acting
Tiotropium  → Long-acting
```

* **Ipratropium:** SAMA — short-acting muscarinic antagonist
* **Tiotropium:** LAMA — long-acting muscarinic antagonist

⏱️ **Approximate duration:**

* Ipratropium: approximately **4–6 hours**
* Tiotropium: approximately **24 hours**

🧠 **Memory:** *Ipratropium is Immediate and shorter; Tiotropium Takes longer.*

---

### 2. Formoterol and Salmeterol

```text
F comes before S

Formoterol → Faster onset
Salmeterol → Slower onset
```

Formoterol and salmeterol are **both long-acting β₂-agonists—LABAs**.

⏱️ **Approximate timing:**

* Formoterol onset: approximately **5 minutes**
* Salmeterol onset: approximately **30 minutes**
* Duration of both medicines: approximately **12 hours**

🧠 **Memory:** *Formoterol is Fast; Salmeterol Starts Slowly.*

⚠️ This comparison concerns **onset**, not duration. Formoterol is not a short-acting medicine.

---

### 3. Insulin Aspart and Insulin Glargine

```text
A comes before G

Aspart   → Rapid-acting
Glargine → Long-acting
```

⏱️ **Approximate timing:**

* Insulin aspart:

  * Meal should generally follow within **5–10 minutes**
  * Peak glucose-lowering effect: approximately **1–3 hours**
  * Duration: approximately **3–5 hours**

* Insulin glargine:

  * Gradual onset
  * Duration: approximately **24 hours or longer**, depending on the formulation and patient
  * U-300 insulin glargine develops its onset over approximately **6 hours**

🧠 **Memory:** *Aspart Acts ASAP; Glargine Goes Gradually.*

⚠️ Insulin timing varies considerably with formulation, dose, injection site, exercise and individual response.

---

### 4. Alteplase and Tenecteplase

```text
A comes before T

Alteplase    → Shorter half-life
Tenecteplase → Longer half-life
```

⏱️ **Approximate elimination half-life:**

* Alteplase initial half-life: **less than 5 minutes**
* Tenecteplase initial half-life: approximately **20–24 minutes**
* Tenecteplase terminal half-life: approximately **90–130 minutes**

🧠 **Memory:** *Alteplase goes Away earlier; Tenecteplase Takes longer.*

⚠️ Initial and terminal half-lives are different pharmacokinetic measurements and should not be confused.

---

### 5. Filgrastim and Pegfilgrastim

```text
F comes before P

Filgrastim    → Shorter-acting
Pegfilgrastim → Prolonged-acting
```

⏱️ **Approximate elimination half-life:**

* Filgrastim: approximately **3.5 hours**
* Pegfilgrastim: approximately **15–80 hours**

🧠 **Memory:** *PEG means Prolonged Effect.*

⚠️ Pegfilgrastim clearance depends partly on the neutrophil count, so its half-life is variable.

---

### 6. T3 and T4

```text
3 comes before 4

T3 → Shorter half-life
T4 → Longer half-life
```

⏱️ **Approximate elimination half-life:**

* T3: approximately **1–1.4 days**
* T4: approximately **6–7.5 days**

🧠 **Memory:** *T3 is Fast and Fleeting; T4 Stays for Longer.*

---

### 7. Lidocaine and Ropivacaine

```text
L comes before R

Lidocaine   → Generally shorter duration
Ropivacaine → Generally longer duration
```

⏱️ **Example timing from selected regional-anaesthesia techniques:**

* Lidocaine: approximately **1–1.5 hours**
* Ropivacaine: approximately **3.7–8.7 hours**

🧠 **Memory:** *Lidocaine Leaves earlier; Ropivacaine Remains longer.*

⚠️ These figures are not universal. Local-anaesthetic onset and duration vary substantially with the dose, concentration, injection site, type of nerve block and use of adrenaline or other additives.

---

## 🎯 One-Line Revision

```text
I → T    Ipratropium: 4–6 h; Tiotropium: ~24 h
F → S    Formoterol onset: ~5 min; Salmeterol: ~30 min
A → G    Aspart: 3–5 h; Glargine: ~24 h or longer
A → T    Alteplase t½: <5 min; Tenecteplase: longer
F → P    Filgrastim t½: ~3.5 h; Pegfilgrastim: 15–80 h
3 → 4    T3 t½: 1–1.4 days; T4: 6–7.5 days
L → R    Lidocaine: ~1–1.5 h; Ropivacaine: ~3.7–8.7 h
```

> **Remember the pattern—but always understand the pharmacology behind it.**

### Important Note

This is a **selective memory aid**, not a universal pharmacological rule. Alphabetical or numerical order does not determine the onset, duration or half-life of every medicine.

The terms **onset**, **duration of clinical action** and **elimination half-life** do not mean the same thing. Always verify medicine information using current product information and authoritative clinical references.
